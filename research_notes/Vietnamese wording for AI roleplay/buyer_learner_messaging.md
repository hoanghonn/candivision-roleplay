# Messaging that persuades Vietnamese business buyers and working adult learners (AI role-play training)

How to read these notes (researched 2026-11-01):
- Evidence tags: [Survey], [Peer-reviewed], [Regulation], [Press], [Vendor page] (what a seller chooses to say, not proof that it works), [Sponsored/opinion], [Self-published].
- [Snippet] means the wording came from a search result and the page itself was not opened. Check it before quoting it publicly.
- Quotes were extracted with a fetch tool, not copied by hand. Re-check any quote before it goes into marketing. Em dashes in originals are shown as a normal dash.
- Anything older than 2023 is labelled OLDER. Wording marked "untested proposal" is my opinion, not evidence.
- The biggest limit: I found no A/B test, conversion data or message-testing study for Vietnamese B2B or EdTech, and no study of Vietnamese adults' reactions to specific result labels. Sections on what "works" rest on what vendors choose to publish and on adjacent research.

## 1. What do Vietnamese buyers of corporate training and HR software care about most?

### Takeaway
No source I found ranks buyer priorities directly, so there is no survey-backed order. The best indirect signal is what Vietnamese vendors put first: customer counts, named logos, a free trial, security certificates and specific savings. The strongest buyer-side signal is doubt: only 5.4% of firms strongly trust AI output, and 20% of HR respondents doubt AI's profitability.

### Cited Findings
Buyer attitude data
- [Survey] Only "Chỉ 5,4% doanh nghiệp đặt mức rất tin tưởng vào kết quả AI" and use it with almost no manual review; n=846 online responses from C-level, HR, recruitment and IT people, June to July 2025 - [ITviec](https://itviec.com/blog/tom-tat-chinh-bao-cao-ung-dung-ai-va-tuyen-dung-it-tai-vietnam/)
- [Survey] Talentnet and VNHR Club "AI Reality Check", n=741 (89% in HR), announced 15/10/2025: 91% have used AI at work, but "chỉ hơn 50% cảm thấy ứng dụng này thực sự mang lại hiệu quả". 61% call AI "con dao hai lưỡi", 40% believe risks can be controlled with clear direction, 20% doubt its profitability - [Doanh nghiệp và Hội nhập](https://doanhnghiephoinhap.vn/doanh-nghiep-viet-chi-14-ung-dung-ai-hieu-qua-118958.html)
- [Survey] In the same study, 90% have or are building internal AI rules, but "chỉ 14% cho biết các quy định này được áp dụng hiệu quả vào thực tế". - [Self-published, global figure] "46% doanh nghiệp không thể tính chính xác ROI của các sáng kiến đào tạo" (Training Industry, cited by a Vietnamese HR vendor). Caution on case studies
- [Vendor page] MISA's BHL Group case study has no measured result and no customer quote. It lists problems, then celebrates the kick-off - [MISA AMIS](https://amis.misa.vn/cau-chuyen-khach-hang/?p=1213)
- [Snippet] MISA's own pages give different customer totals (about 170,000, 200,000, 250,000 and 350,000). Self-reported counts are unaudited and inconsistent - [MISA solutions page](https://amis.misa.vn/?p=55165); [MISA case study page](https://amis.misa.vn/cau-chuyen-khach-hang/?p=1213)

### Inferences
- Likely proof order for a new vendor with no large customer count: (1) a named Vietnamese pilot with one before-and-after number, (2) a free trial or a pilot on the buyer's own scenarios, (3) data security, (4) price. This is my judgement from vendor pages plus the trust data, not a measured ranking.
- Two audiences need different openings. The owner wants results in money and staff retention ("Con số cho CFO"). The training lead also needs something to defend upward and must show that it measures skill, not attendance.
- "We check the research" has a real opening because buyers already distrust AI output. It should be framed as "you do not have to take the AI's word for it" rather than as an academic credential.
- Do not copy the seat-count style of proof until the number is real and exact. An unsupportable "#1" is a risk for a small brand.
- MISA's BHL case shows a named local story with no numbers is an accepted format here, so a single measured result would stand out.
- The SaaS adoption paper's emphasis on top management support suggests the owner or director is the deciding factor, which matches the brief.

### Gaps
- No Vietnam survey ranks cost, proof, references, ROI, trust and speed for training or HR software buyers. Searches for Anphabe, Deloitte and Base.vn material on this found nothing usable.
- The Base.vn page returned only its headline, and the Gitiho business page returned 403, so neither could be analysed.
- No data on typical purchase process, price tolerance or sales cycle for Vietnamese L&D software. Customer interviews are the realistic way to get this.

## 2. How does face affect practice and feedback, and what wording reassures?

### Takeaway
Reluctance to speak because of face is well described for Vietnamese settings, and one large Vietnamese study suggests AI practice helps, but that study covers university English learners rather than employees. "Private" is the best-supported reassurance. "Không ai đánh giá bạn" would be false for a product whose AI judges evaluate the call.

### Cited Findings
Evidence
- [Peer-reviewed] Hoang (2025), Australasian Journal of Educational Technology 41(3): 16-week quasi-experiment, 240 Vietnamese second-year university students across four institutions, speaking proficiency gain d = 1.11 against the control group. The author concludes AI tools create "low-stakes practice environments that circumvent cultural barriers to speaking participation" - [AJET](https://ajet.org.au/index.php/AJET/article/download/10050/2185/34379)
- [Peer-reviewed, interpretation] From the same paper: "The AI environment appears to have created a psychological safe space outside traditional hierarchical structures, allowing practice without fear of judgement." The study measured proficiency and engagement. In the extracted text, "anxiety" appears only once, in the introduction, so this looks like the author's explanation and not a measured result about face - [AJET](https://ajet.org.au/index.php/AJET/article/download/10050/2185/34379)
- [Peer-reviewed, secondary] The same paper cites H. T. Nguyen (2020, OLDER), ethnographic work in five Vietnamese universities, attributing reluctance to speak to "cultural factors such as face-saving concerns and respect for authority" - [AJET](https://ajet.org.au/index.php/AJET/article/download/10050/2185/34379)
- [Doctoral thesis, OLDER, 2018] Tran, Leiden: "sense of time and face-concern are the two main grounds for conflicts" between Western and Vietnamese professionals - [Leiden](https://scholarlypublications.universiteitleiden.nl/access/item%3A2971145/view)
- [Snippet, preprint] A qualitative study of FPT University students reportedly links a habit of being judged negatively to unwillingness to speak. Only the title could be confirmed: "Foreign Language Speaking Anxiety Among Vietnamese EFL Students at FPT University" - [Research Square](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-10370302/latest)

Real learner language
- [Vendor page, testimonial] On Prep's AI role-play product: "Đã từng đi học tiếng Anh ở các trung tâm nhưng hướng nội nên mình chả bao giờ nói nhiều trên lớp." and "Học với PrepTalk đỡ bị tâm lý, thấy tự tin nói hơn" (Phương Uyên). This is the closest match found to the target benefit, in a learner's own words, though it was selected by the vendor - [Prep](https://prepedu.com/vi)
- [Vendor page, testimonial] "Nhờ AI chấm bài, em nhận ra lỗi sai và cải thiện khả năng học tiếng Trung hiệu quả hơn." - [Prep](https://prepedu.com/vi)

Practitioner opinion (not evidence)
- [Sponsored/opinion, 25/03/2026] "Public criticism can lead to loss of face and damage relationships, sometimes irreparably."; "Feedback is most effective when delivered privately, framed constructively, and anchored in respect."; "Silence itself can indicate uncertainty or disagreement." - [Vietnam Briefing, sponsored post](https://www.vietnam-briefing.com/news/managing-vietnamese-teams-decoding-culture-to-build-trust-performance-and-long-term-engagement.html)
- [Snippet, HR blog] "Nhân viên thiếu cảm giác an toàn sẽ giữ im lặng và không dám bày tỏ ý kiến." - [JobOKO](https://vn.joboko.com/blog/psychological-safety-la-gi-nwi2502)
- [Snippet, HR blog] Staff stick to safe behaviour for fear of being noticed by superiors or seen by colleagues as "chơi trội" - [CareerLink](https://www.careerlink.vn/cam-nang-tuyen-dung/nghe-thuat-quan-ly/5-li-do-nhan-vien-khong-ra-khoi-vung-an-toan-khi-lam-viec)
- [Press, title only; page returned 403] Vietnamese HR writing uses this exact pairing: "từ 'sợ bị đánh giá' đến 'dám thể hiện bản thân'" - [Brands Vietnam](https://www.brandsvietnam.com/congdong/topic/hr-talks-3-psychological-safety-va-hanh-trinh-tu-so-bi-danh-gia-den-dam-the-hien-ban-than)
- [Snippet] Viện IMT has a national psychological safety survey for Vietnam 2025. The page describes the survey but gives no results - [Brands Vietnam](https://www.brandsvietnam.com/congdong/topic/buc-tranh-an-toan-tam-ly-trong-moi-truong-lam-viec-tai-viet-nam-2025)

### Inferences
- Prep's "Nhờ AI chấm bài" testimonial suggests being marked by AI is not automatically threatening when the learner chooses it. An employer-provided tool may feel different.
- The fear is about people: the manager, colleagues and looking foolish in a group. So reassurance should name the audience that is absent, and it need not claim that nothing evaluates the learner.
- Honesty constraint. "Không ai đánh giá bạn" contradicts the two AI judges. "Không ai xem" is only safe if it is true for the training lead too. A search of docs/FACTS.md and README.md found no statement on who can see a call, so confirm the actual visibility rules before writing this claim. If leads can see results, say exactly what they see. A privacy claim that later proves false costs more trust than no claim at all.
- Untested proposals for learners, each conditional on the product's actual visibility settings:
  - "Luyện riêng một mình với nhân vật AI, không có đồng nghiệp hay cấp trên trong phòng."
  - "Nói sai ở đây để không nói sai với khách hàng."
  - "Luyện lại bao nhiêu lần tùy bạn."
  - "Kết quả chỉ để bạn luyện tập, không dùng để tuyển dụng, cho thôi việc hay xếp loại nhân viên."
- The Vietnamese word for "judge" needs care. "Giám khảo" and "chấm điểm" sound like an exam, while "phán xét" sounds moralising. "Hai góc nhìn nhận xét" or "hai AI nhận xét" fits better, and "nhận xét" is the standard school-feedback word. This is my language judgement and is untested.

### Gaps
- No study of Vietnamese employees (as opposed to students) practising with AI, and none directly measuring face or fear of judgement as an outcome.
- No data on whether employees believe a "private" claim when their employer pays for and administers the tool. This is probably the key objection and needs pilot interviews.

## 3. How should negative results be worded? Is "Chưa đạt" acceptable?

### Takeaway
"Chưa đạt" is acceptable and not unusually harsh: it is the official word in Vietnam's secondary-school assessment scale. The risk is that it sounds like a school report, so use it per criterion rather than on the whole person, and always pair it with a specific next step. No evidence was found on how adults react to it.

### Cited Findings
- [Regulation, via press, OLDER 2021, still relevant] Circular 22/2021: "Thông tư 22 đánh giá kết quả học tập của người học theo 4 mức: Tốt, Khá, Đạt, Chưa đạt", and comment-only subjects use "hai mức Đạt, Chưa đạt" - [VnEconomy](https://vneconomy.vn/hoc-sinh-thcs-va-thpt-bo-danh-gia-bang-diem-so-chuyen-sang-danh-gia-bang-nhan-xet.htm)
- [Regulation, via legal explainer] Circular 27/2020 (primary school) uses "Hoàn thành tốt", "Hoàn thành" and "Chưa hoàn thành" for subjects. For qualities and competencies it uses T (Tốt), Đ (Đạt) and C, where C means "Cần cố gắng". Comments note what needs "khắc phục, giúp đỡ" and "hạn chế hoặc khuyến nghị" - [LuatVietnam](https://luatvietnam.vn/linh-vuc-khac/huong-dan-nhan-xet-hoc-ba-theo-thong-tu-27-2020-tt-bgddt-883-97745-article.html)
- [Regulation, quoted by explainer] Circular 27 Article 4 asks teachers to "coi trọng việc động viên, khuyến khích sự cố gắng trong học tập, rèn luyện của học sinh", and "không so sánh học sinh này với học sinh khác, không tạo áp lực cho học sinh, giáo viên và cha mẹ học sinh" - [Thư viện Nhà đất](https://thuviennhadat.vn/phap-ly-nha-dat/loi-nhan-xet-mon-toan-lop-2-theo-thong-tu-27-677463.html)
- [Sample comments, third-party template site] Honest weak-result wording in use: "Em còn nhầm lẫn trong phép trừ và chia, cần ôn luyện thêm về các phép tính."; "Em cần rèn thêm kỹ năng đọc hiểu đề toán để tránh nhầm lẫn." The pattern is a specific skill, then "còn" (still), then "cần ... thêm" (needs more) - [Thư viện Nhà đất](https://thuviennhadat.vn/phap-ly-nha-dat/loi-nhan-xet-mon-toan-lop-2-theo-thong-tu-27-677463.html)
- [Vendor page] An EdTech and an LMS use progress language around assessment: "Luyện tập khẩu ngữ cùng AI, tiến bộ rõ nét qua từng bài." [Prep](https://prepedu.com/vi); "ĐÁNH GIÁ KẾT QUẢ, THÚC ĐẨY TIẾN BỘ" [Unica LMS](https://lms.unica.vn/)
- [Project file] "Chưa đạt" currently appears in the product's own strings - candivision-roleplay/src/locales/vi.json (local file, usage not reviewed)

### Inferences
- "Chưa" means "not yet" and "không" means "not", so "Chưa đạt" already implies the learner can get there, while "Không đạt" sounds final. This is my knowledge of the language, not a sourced finding.
- Recommendation: keep "Chưa đạt" as the honest label, but apply it to a criterion and not to the person or the whole call. Replacing it with something vaguer would undercut the product's "honest, checked" position.
- Untested proposals:
  - Criterion result: "Xử lý phản đối: Chưa đạt"
  - Next step: "Cần luyện thêm: nhắc lại mối lo của khách trước khi trả lời" (adapts the school pattern "cần ôn luyện thêm")
  - Time-bound headline for a call: "Lần này còn 2 điểm cần luyện thêm"
  - Action: "Luyện lại đoạn này"
- Softer labels, by fit for adults:
  - "Cần luyện thêm": best fit. Honest, action-based, and close to real usage.
  - "Chưa hoàn thành": officially used, but better suited to tasks than to skills.
  - "Cần cố gắng": real and official, but it comes from primary-school records and may feel patronising to an adult. Avoid it.
  - "Cần cải thiện": plausible, but I did not verify its use in a Vietnamese product.
- Avoid "Không đạt", "Thất bại", "Kém", "Yếu" and the pronoun "em" in adult-facing feedback.
- Circular 27's "no comparing students" principle supports leaving out leaderboards. So does the regulatory point in section 6.
- Quick check worth doing: show 5 to 8 employees the same result under "Chưa đạt", "Cần luyện thêm" and "Cần cố gắng". It is cheap and fills the main gap in this section.

### Gaps
- No evidence on adult or employee reactions to "Chưa đạt" versus the alternatives.
- Could not verify score labels inside ELSA or other adult products: the ELSA Vietnam page returned 404 and only third-party descriptions were available.
- The Circular 22 and 27 originals were not read. Wording comes from press and legal explainers, and the grade rollout for Circular 22 varies between sources.

## 4. Do "based on research" claims work, or do local cases and named companies work better?

### Takeaway
No direct test exists. Vendor pages lead with local customers and outcomes, and none of the B2B pages I read led with research. Foreign research is not rejected outright, though: Vietnamese L&D content cites it often, usually to support a business point. So use research as the supporting reason behind a practical result, never as the headline.

### Cited Findings
- [Vendor page] Prep's proof section opens "Chất lượng được minh chứng bởi" and then shows outcomes and people: "Hàng nghìn gương mặt xuất sắc", "100.000+ học viên", "119 HỌC VIÊN ĐIỂM CAO IELTS THÁNG 8 CỦA PREP". Method appears only as a passing reason in a blog: "nhờ hiệu ứng giãn cách trong ghi nhớ" - [Prep](https://prepedu.com/vi)
- [Vendor page] MISA, Unica and Lạc Việt prove themselves with customer counts, logos, renewal rate and certificates, with no research claim among the items extracted - [MISA AMIS](https://amis.misa.vn/ld/giai-phap-quan-tri-kenh-phan-phoi-2in1); [Unica LMS](https://lms.unica.vn/); [Lạc Việt](https://lacviet.vn/en/lac-viet-e-learning-dao-tao-doanh-nghiep/)
- [Self-published, counter-example] A Vietnamese HR vendor's report for local buyers rests heavily on foreign sources by name: LinkedIn WLR 2025, Training Industry, ATD, Udemy Business. Foreign data is used as credible when tied to a business outcome such as retention - [Tanca](https://tanca.io/baocao2026/Tanca_Vietnam_Corporate_Learning_Report_2026.pdf)
- [Snippet, OLDER, 2015, consumer] Nielsen: 89% of Vietnamese consumers follow word-of-mouth recommendations from people they know, and "93 per cent" act on these opinions most of the time. This was the most trusted source, but it is old and not B2B - [Vietnam News](https://bizhub.vietnamnews.vn/nielsen-vietnamese-customers-trust-friends-over-ads-post2524.html)
- [Survey] Only 5.4% of firms strongly trust AI output, and most review it manually - [ITviec](https://itviec.com/blog/tom-tat-chinh-bao-cao-ung-dung-ai-va-tuyen-dung-it-tai-vietnam/)

### Inferences
- Claim likely to feel foreign or academic: "Dựa trên nghiên cứu khoa học" on its own. It is generic and gives the reader nothing to check.
- More credible pattern: name the concrete behaviour, then the source, then the human check. Untested proposal: "Mỗi nhận xét đều gắn với đúng khoảnh khắc trong cuộc gọi và đối chiếu với nguồn nghiên cứu đã kiểm tra - bạn tự xem được vì sao." This turns research into something verifiable, which suits buyers who already review AI output by hand.
- Moments tied to feedback are probably a stronger proof point than "research", because a buyer can see them in a 10-minute demo.
- Before there are local references, the honest substitute is a pilot on the buyer's own scripts or a demo with a Vietnamese scenario. Do not imply customers that do not yet exist. The first two or three named references should be a top commercial priority.

### Gaps
- No study or test compares research claims with local case studies among Vietnamese buyers.
- No recent (2023 onwards) Vietnam data on trust in recommendations, and none specific to B2B. The 2015 Nielsen figures are the best found.

## 5. Headline styles and calls to action, with verbatim examples

### Takeaway
Vietnamese B2B pages in this sample mostly use category-plus-proof headlines and low-commitment calls to action (free trial, free consultation, ebook, Zalo chat). EdTech is more outcome and emotion-led. Almost no page opens with an explicit pain statement, so a good pain-led headline could stand out, but this is untested.

### Inferences
These are opinion, and none has been tested.
- CTA recommendation for buyers: "Đăng ký dùng thử" or "Đăng ký tư vấn miễn phí", plus a Zalo option. Zalo appears on the Unica page and is a low-friction channel. For a stronger offer: "Xem thử với kịch bản của công ty bạn."
- CTA for learners: "Bắt đầu luyện" (short, matches Prep's style).
- Candidate buyer headlines:
  - Pain-led: "Nhân viên học xong kịch bản, nhưng vẫn lúng túng khi khách từ chối?"
  - Outcome-led: "Cho nhân viên luyện những cuộc gọi khó trước khi gặp khách thật."
  - Trust-led: "Nhận xét bằng AI có căn cứ, gắn với đúng khoảnh khắc trong cuộc gọi."
- Likely to sound salesy for this product: "#1", "bứt phá", "đột phá", "toàn diện nhất", "cách mạng", "siêu xịn", and all-caps blocks. These are common in the category, so they would not differentiate a product built on being checked and honest.
- Likely to sound translated:
  - "Giải pháp hàng đầu dành cho..."
  - "Trao quyền cho nhân viên" (from "empower")
  - "Hành trình học tập" used loosely
  - "Trải nghiệm" used as a filler verb
  - Direct calques of "role-play" and "hard conversations": prefer "tình huống khó" or "cuộc nói chuyện khó". "Nhập vai" is safe because Prep already uses it.
- Word order matters. Natural Vietnamese puts the concrete situation first and avoids long noun chains. A native reviewer should check every headline, because I found no source on what reads as translated.

### Gaps
- No conversion or A/B data for any headline or CTA style in Vietnam. All of the above reflects what sellers publish.
- Small sample: four usable B2B or EdTech pages. Base.vn gave only a headline, the Lạc Việt Vietnamese version was not read, and Gitiho and ELSA Vietnam could not be fetched.
- No source documents which phrases Vietnamese buyers perceive as translated or salesy.

## 6. Reactions to AI in training and HR, including machine scoring and privacy

### Takeaway
Vietnamese workers are heavy, fairly positive AI users, so general fear of AI is unlikely to be the main barrier. I found no independent survey on fear of machine scoring. The regulatory picture has changed sharply: automated assessment and ranking of learners is on Vietnam's high-risk list for education, which makes "for training only, no rankings" a selling point and also a legal question.

### Cited Findings
Attitudes and use
- [Survey, via press] PwC Vietnam Hopes and Fears 2025 (launched 6/2/2026, over 1,000 workers): "83% người lao động Việt Nam sử dụng AI trong công việc, vượt xa mức trung bình toàn cầu là 69%"; daily generative AI use 38% against 14% globally; "90% người dùng ghi nhận sự cải thiện rõ rệt về năng suất và chất lượng". PwC's own PDF returned 403 - [VnEconomy](https://vneconomy.vn/lao-dong-viet-nam-su-dung-ai-vuot-xa-muc-trung-binh-toan-cau.htm)
- [Survey, via press] Same study: "58% đang trong trạng thái mệt mỏi"; Gen Z shows the most anxiety about future job stability (no figure given) - [VnEconomy](https://vneconomy.vn/lao-dong-viet-nam-su-dung-ai-vuot-xa-muc-trung-binh-toan-cau.htm)
- [Survey] 61% of HR-heavy respondents call AI "con dao hai lưỡi" - [Doanh nghiệp và Hội nhập](https://doanhnghiephoinhap.vn/doanh-nghiep-viet-chi-14-ung-dung-ai-hieu-qua-118958.html)

Scoring and monitoring
- [Snippet, secondary, OLDER, unverified] A trade article cites MISA (2022) saying 58% of firms use AI to monitor and evaluate staff, and PwC Vietnam (2022) saying 40% of employees fear AI will replace their job. Neither was traced to the original - [Tạp chí Công Thương](https://tapchicongthuong.vn/ung-dung-ai-trong-quan-ly-nguon-nhan-luc-153302.htm)
- [Snippet, international, country unclear] Qin et al. (2023) reportedly found employees rated AI as fairer and more accurate than the average human manager. Relevance to Vietnam is unconfirmed - [SUFE newsletter](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/407700)
- [Snippet, vendor blog] Fear of information leaks may make staff unwilling to cooperate with HR software. This is opinion, not measurement - [MISA AMIS](https://amis.misa.vn/210351/kho-khan-trong-quan-ly-nhan-su/)

Law
- [Press] AI Law No. 134/2025/QH15 was passed on 10/12/2025 and took effect on 1/3/2026 - [Apolat Legal](https://apolatlegal.com/vi/laws/luat-tri-tue-nhan-tao-2025-so-134-2025-qh15/)
- [Law firm] Decision 33/2026 took effect on 15/8/2026 and "lists 46 high-risk AI systems across six sectors": education, ethnic and religious affairs, healthcare, banking, judicial proceedings and transportation. Labour or HR is not a named sector. Education items include systems that "automatically conduct examinations, assess learning outcomes or rank learners", and systems that monitor and analyse learner behaviour using biometric data or other automated monitoring. Existing education systems must comply by 1/9/2027. The article does not say whether corporate use is covered - [Allen & Gledhill](https://www.allenandgledhill.com/sg/publication/articles/33374/identifies-high-risk-ai-systems-across-six-sectors)
- [Press] Vietnamese wording: "hệ thống tự động kiểm tra, đánh giá kết quả, xếp hạng người học"; "hệ thống AI giám sát, phân tích hành vi người học" - [VOV](https://vov.vn/cong-nghe/ban-hanh-danh-muc-he-thong-ai-rui-ro-cao-trong-6-linh-vuc-post1311806.vov)
- [Legal press] Personal Data Protection Law No. 91/2025/QH15 took effect on 1/1/2026; it bars asking for data beyond the lawful recruitment purpose and restricts sensitive employee data - [LSVN](https://lsvn.vn/nhung-du-lieu-doanh-nghiep-khong-duoc-thu-thap-khi-tuyen-dung-quan-ly-nhan-su-a169304.html); [Cần Thơ legal portal](https://pbgdpl.cantho.gov.vn/quy-dinh-ve-bao-ve-du-lieu-ca-nhan-trong-tuyen-dung-quan-ly-su-dung-nguoi-lao-dong)
- [Legal press] Headline: "Doanh nghiệp trí tuệ nhân tạo không được thu thập dữ liệu cá nhân vượt quá mục đích" - [LuatVietnam](https://luatvietnam.vn/tin-van-ban-moi/doanh-nghiep-tri-tue-nhan-tao-chi-duoc-xu-ly-du-lieu-ca-nhan-trong-pham-vi-can-thiet-186-103759-article.html)
- [Survey] Only 7% of firms plan to strengthen AI governance and risk management - [ITviec](https://itviec.com/blog/tom-tat-chinh-bao-cao-ung-dung-ai-va-tuyen-dung-it-tai-vietnam/)

### Inferences
- Qin et al., if it applies here, suggests AI scoring is not necessarily seen as less fair than a manager. For staff who distrust a manager's judgement, that could even be a plus.
- ITviec's 7% suggests most buyers will not have an AI governance process ready, so a clear data and use-limits sheet could save them work.
- Message implication: do not spend copy persuading Vietnamese staff that AI is acceptable. Spend it on purpose and limits: what is stored, who sees it, and what it will never be used for.
- "Never for hiring, firing or performance ratings" should be promoted. It answers the job-security worry for learners and the purpose-limitation question for buyers. To carry weight it should appear in the contract and product settings, not only on the marketing page.
- Legal flag, which needs counsel: two AI judges evaluating "người học" could be read as close to the Decision 33 education item, even though I found nothing saying workplace training falls under the education sector. A voice-analysing product should also check the monitoring item. Wording choices ("chấm điểm", "xếp hạng", "giám sát") and features (leaderboards, manager rankings) could affect how the product is perceived or classified. This is my reading of secondary summaries, not legal advice.
- Reassurance wording for buyers (untested): "Không bán dữ liệu, không dùng cuộc gọi của nhân viên cho mục đích khác ngoài luyện tập" - only if this is true of the product and of its AI providers. Include where data is stored only once that has been verified.

### Gaps
- No independent Vietnam survey on employees' attitudes to being scored by AI, or on voice-recording privacy in training.
- Did not read the original text of Law 134/2025, Law 91/2025 or Decision 33/2026, so whether corporate training is in scope remains open.
- No documented case found of a Vietnamese backlash against AI scoring in training.

- The sales and call-centre entry story, "scripts are not enough when customers push back and new staff leave within months", fits the press evidence. Do not quote a Vietnamese turnover percentage, because none was found.

### Gaps
