# Vietnamese wording used by AI speaking and practice products

Accessed 2026-11-01. Quotes in "double quotes" are verbatim. Store listings, Prep pages, ELSA pages and Duolingo strings were taken from raw page text, not from a summariser. Where a quote is from a search snippet only, it says so.

Evidence types, in order of strength for interface wording:
- UI = a real interface string (only Duolingo, from its public web bundle, plus ELSA's own help page quoting its screens).
- Help = vendor help or walk-through describing the interface.
- Marketing = store listing or landing page.
- Secondary = press or a third-party article.

Known typos in the sources, do not copy them: ELSA homepage "Nhận phản hồi hồi thì" and "stub hội thoại"; ELSA help "chủ để" (for chủ đề); Speak App Store "gọi ý" (for gợi ý).

## How do these products word each idea in Vietnamese?

### Takeaway
There is a usable shared vocabulary: "Bắt đầu", "Luyện tập", "Luyện nói", "nhập vai", "tình huống", "phản hồi", "kết quả", "Thử lại", "Tiếp tục", "tiến độ". Two concepts have no verified wording in any product: "hold to talk" and "not yet passing". Those must not be presented as market-standard.

### Inferences
- "Chưa có ..." is a natural pattern for empty lists, and "Chưa ..." also carries the softer "not yet" sense, but I found it for lists, not for scores. A "Chưa đạt" result label would be this team's own choice, not market wording.
- For the microphone, "Bấm" (ELSA) and "Nhấn" (Prep, Duolingo) both occur. Any "hold" wording would be new to the category as far as this sample shows, and should be tested with users.

### Gaps
- Hold to talk: not found. Most apps sit behind a login, and their in-app text and screenshots are not readable as text. Needs a hands-on check in the apps.
- Pass or fail labels: not found. Same limitation.
- Empty states: only Duolingo. ELSA, Prep, Speak, Praktika and Loora in-app empty screens could not be verified.
- Direct in-app strings for ELSA, Prep, Speak, Praktika and Loora were not available. Their wording here is marketing or help text.

## Which verbs and nouns recur for practice, role-play, feedback and coach, and in what setting?

### Takeaway
"Luyện tập" and "luyện nói" lead for practice, with "thực hành" concentrated in Speak's copy and PrepTalk. "Nhập vai" is the favoured name for the feature, "tình huống" or "bối cảnh" for the situation, and "kịch bản" appears mainly in B2B and workplace copy. "Phản hồi" is the default for feedback. Coach splits by vendor between "gia sư" and "huấn luyện viên".

## What forms of address do they use, and how do they word failure or low scores?

### Takeaway
"Bạn" is the near-universal form of address. I found no "em" or "anh/chị" addressing the learner in the sampled copy. Failure is rarely named. Products talk about "lỗi sai", "điểm cần cải thiện" and "chưa đúng", paired with a concrete fix or encouragement, often softened with "nhé".

- "Bạn" is the safe default, and a role-play product can address employees with it without seeming over-familiar. The AI character in a scenario may need other forms such as "anh/chị" or "em", because Vietnamese address depends on the relationship. No sampled product documents how it handles that, so this is my reasoning and not a finding.
- The recurring pattern for a weak result is: say it softly, name the specific gap, give one next action, close with "nhé". "Điểm mạnh" paired with "điểm cần cải thiện" is the cleanest wording for feedback I found, in two different settings (ELSA uses "điểm yếu", MoMo uses "điểm cần cải thiện").
- "Chưa" works as the softener ("chưa đúng", "chưa chính xác", "chưa tốt"), which supports a "not yet" framing, though as noted no product uses it as a pass or fail label.
- Duolingo's cheer ("Thất bại cay đắng!", proverbs) suits a game. It may feel off for employees practising a hard conversation at work.

### Gaps
- No product's real low-score screen could be read. Failure wording above is from interface strings, help pages and marketing, not from observed result screens, apart from Duolingo's strings.
- How AI characters address learners inside a Vietnamese conversation was not verifiable. Most of these products practise English, so the character speaks English and the Vietnamese wording covers only the surrounding interface. This is a major limit for an app whose conversation itself may be in Vietnamese.

## Do they keep English loanwords, and which ones?

### Takeaway
Yes, freely, and in a consistent pattern. "AI" is universal. "Feedback" is translated almost everywhere I looked, while "demo", "avatar", "streak", exam terms and branded feature names stay in English. "Role-play" is usually translated in running text but kept in feature names.

### Inferences
- Vietnamese products selling to English learners can use more English because their users are studying it and expect exam terms. An employee soft-skills app has no such reason, so Prep's level of mixing is a poor model, and Duolingo's mostly translated interface is a better one.
- Keep: "AI", "demo", "CV". Prefer Vietnamese: "phản hồi", "nhập vai", "tình huống". "Coach" as a label is proven in product names only. In body text the sampled products use "huấn luyện viên".
- Foreign vendors' store copy (Praktika, Loora, parts of Speak) reads as if translated, for example Loora's "Nó luôn có sẵn" and "ngại mắc phải". Treat their phrasing as evidence of terms in circulation rather than as a model of natural Vietnamese. This is my reading of the text.
