# How Vietnamese corporate trainers talk about practising hard conversations

Evidence tags. No tag: the quote was string-matched against the text of the page I downloaded. (W): extracted by a fetch tool, so the exact wording is likely but I did not string-match it. (S): seen only in a search-result snippet or summary, because the page blocked direct access. Treat (S) as a lead, not as confirmed wording.

Coverage limits that apply to everything below:
- 66 Vietnamese-language pages were saved and searched. It is not a random sample, and many are content marketing by software or call-centre vendors rather than trainers' own materials. Counts show what appeared in this set, not market share.
- The named premium providers mostly gave nothing usable. Dale Carnegie Vietnam and VinUni returned 403. The guessed BizLIGHT .vn and Mi Group addresses did not connect, and bizlight.edu.vn returned 503. The PACE pages I could open had no Vietnamese course copy, and the Professional Selling Skills URL returned 404. Talentnet and the Tâm Việt homepage had no matching course wording. Most verbatim course wording therefore comes from OCD, HRD Academy, iRTC, IFA, PMS, Vân Nguyên Edubiz, CiCC and similar providers.
- Web pages show how providers sell and describe training. They do not show what a trainer says aloud in class or what internal company forms say.

## 1. How do Vietnamese trainers name role-play in class, and which term is most common for corporate training?

### Takeaway
The most verified corporate wording is built on "thực hành" plus "tình huống" (practising situations), often beside the English "Role Play". "Đóng vai" is the plain Vietnamese verb and "nhập vai" also appears, while "sắm vai" and "tập dượt" did not appear on any saved page.

### Inferences
- For product labels, "thực hành tình huống" looks like the safest umbrella and "đóng vai" the clearest way to explain the mechanic. Sellers often gloss the idea with the English term, which suggests "Role Play" is understood by corporate buyers.
- "Sắm vai" and "tập dượt" may exist elsewhere. They are simply absent here, and an exact-term search for "sắm vai" surfaced no page that used it.

### Gaps
- No evidence of what trainers say aloud in class. Course pages are written for buyers.
- No reliable frequency data. The counts come from a small set and search tools favoured non-Vietnamese results.
- No Dale Carnegie, VinUni, BizLIGHT, Mi Group or PACE class wording could be verified.

## 2. What are the natural terms for objections, complaints, angry customers, feedback, hard conversations, incidents, discovery, closing and medical reps?

### Takeaway
"Xử lý từ chối", "khách hàng khó tính", "phản hồi", "khai thác nhu cầu" and "trình dược viên" are all well attested. The English-shaped concepts "cuộc trò chuyện khó" and incident communication were not verified as trainer vocabulary, and "thông tin được duyệt" appears only in near forms such as "được phê duyệt" and "đã được duyệt".

### Inferences
- "Cam kết" is normal wording in this market, but the promise that follows may be either a course outcome or a large sales claim. A role-play product can probably stand out with evidence of behaviour change, since the proof seen is mostly clients and testimonials.
- "Thực hành" percentages are a familiar buyer signal, so measured practice time or attempt counts could fit existing expectations. This is untested with buyers.

### Gaps
- No verified Vietnamese wording from Dale Carnegie, PACE, Tâm Việt, BizLIGHT, VinUni, Talentnet or Mi Group on promises for these course types. For PACE and BizLIGHT I saw only search-summary lines about in-house delivery.
- No independently audited before-and-after result for any Vietnamese corporate soft-skills programme.
- No primary pricing or proof documents such as proposals or sales decks, since these are rarely public.
