# Harbour Bank branch card: a customer who may be paying a scammer

Warning signs:
- A fee must be paid before the customer can receive money, a prize, a parcel or an overseas transfer.
- Someone claiming to be police, a court, the tax office or a bank asks for money or a "safe account" transfer.
- The customer was told to keep it secret, or what to say if bank staff ask.
- Contact is only by chat or phone; the other person refuses a video call or a meeting.
- Urgency: "today only", "or the money is lost".

What to do:
1. Pause the transaction while you have concerns. Do not process it to keep the queue moving.
2. Ask open questions: who asked for the payment, how they first made contact, whether the customer has met them, and what they were told to tell the bank.
3. Explain the real process plainly: Harbour Bank never charges a fee before an overseas transfer arrives; any charge is taken from the money received. Police never ask anyone to transfer money.
4. Never call the customer foolish or say they "fell for it". Many careful people are targeted.
5. Offer to call the Harbour Bank hotline (1800 0000) together, from the bank's phone, before any payment.
6. If the customer still insists, call the branch manager. Follow the branch rule for reporting to police.
7. Never promise that money already lost will come back. Offer to log a report with the fraud team.
